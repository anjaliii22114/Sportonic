Hydration basics

Water is involved in almost everything the body does, and we lose some of it through sweat and breathing whenever we are active.

Daily

Drink regularly through the day

Sip water steadily instead of waiting until you are very thirsty. Keeping a bottle within reach makes this easy.

Signs

Watch simple signals

Thirst, a dry mouth, headache or darker-than-usual urine can be signs that you need more fluid.

Weather

Adjust for heat and effort

Hot, humid weather and longer or harder sessions increase sweat loss, so more fluid is usually needed.

Before

Start well hydrated

Have a drink in the hour or two before activity, so you begin comfortably rather than playing catch-up.

During

Small, frequent sips

Drink in small amounts during longer sessions. Drinking a lot at once can feel uncomfortable.

After

Replace what you lost

Drink after activity and have a normal meal, which also helps replace salts the body has lost.

Eating around activity

There is no single perfect diet for active people. These general ideas apply to most people who enjoy regular movement.

WhenGeneral ideaSimple examples
Before activityA light meal or snack that is easy to digest, eaten with enough time to settleBanana, toast, rice or porridge with fruit
During longer activityWater for most sessions, and a small snack only if the session is long and you feel the needFruit pieces, a handful of dates
After activityA balanced meal with some protein and carbohydrates, plus vegetables and fluidLentils and rice, eggs with bread, yogurt and fruit
Every dayA varied plate of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein foods and healthy fatsHome-cooked meals with colourful variety

Food needs vary with age, body size, activity level, culture, health and personal preference. If you have special dietary needs, allergies or a health condition, a qualified dietitian or doctor can give guidance suited to you.

Sleep and recovery

Bodies adapt to activity while resting. A regular sleep routine, rest days and a calm evening help you feel ready for the next session. Most adults do best with a steady sleep schedule, and children and teenagers generally need more sleep than adults.

Light movement such as an easy walk or gentle stretching can feel good on rest days. If you feel unusually tired for several days, it may be a sign to ease off and look at your sleep, food and stress levels.

Habits that support recovery

  • Keep regular bedtimes and wake-up times
  • Wind down away from bright screens before sleep
  • Include at least one lighter day each week
  • Stretch gently and keep drinking water
  • Listen to your body and rest when you need to

Looking at quick-fix claims with care

Advertising often promises fast results from drinks, powders and pills. It helps to pause before believing them.

Be wary of big promises

Claims of instant energy, rapid weight loss or guaranteed performance deserve scepticism. Real change is usually gradual.

Food first

For most casual players, regular meals and water cover what the body needs. Special products are rarely necessary.

Check the label

Look at ingredients, sugar and caffeine content and age warnings. Some products are not suitable for children or certain health conditions.

Be careful with energy drinks

Many contain high levels of caffeine and sugar. Health authorities generally advise caution, particularly for children and teenagers.

Ask a professional

Before taking any supplement, speak to a doctor, pharmacist or dietitian, especially if you take medicines or have a condition.

Trust independent sources

Look for advice from health authorities and qualified professionals rather than sellers of the product.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sportonic a shop, a nutrition service or a supplement brand?

No. Sportonic does not sell drinks, supplements or equipment and does not provide nutrition advice or coaching. It is a reading resource that explains everyday habits in simple language.

Do I need a sports drink for everyday exercise?

For most light or moderate activity, water is enough. Sports drinks are generally considered only for long or very intense sessions, and they contain sugar, so it is wise to read the label.

How do I know if I am drinking enough?

A common simple guide is pale-yellow urine and not feeling very thirsty. Needs vary, so adjust for weather, activity and your own body.

Should I eat before exercising?

Many people feel better with a light snack an hour or two before, while others prefer to exercise on an empty stomach for short sessions. Try what feels comfortable for you.

When should I speak to a professional?

If you have a health condition, special dietary needs, unexplained tiredness, dizziness or any worry about eating or exercise, consult a qualified doctor or dietitian.
